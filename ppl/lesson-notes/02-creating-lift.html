Lesson 2 — Creating Lift

Comprehensive condensed notes for Josh Campbell / Free Pilot Training.

Core idea

  • Lift comes from the wing interacting with relative wind; pilots can change lift by changing airspeed, wing camber/configuration, or angle of attack.
  • The lesson uses both Bernoulli's principle and Newton's third law to explain the aerodynamic force.
  • Changes that increase lift normally change drag and the airplane's energy state too.

Airfoil and Bernoulli

  • An airfoil is a shape designed to obtain an aerodynamic reaction from air moving around it; the wing is an airfoil.
  • Air is accelerated around the curved wing. In the lesson's Bernoulli explanation, faster flow is associated with lower static pressure.
  • The resulting pressure distribution around the airfoil contributes to the upward force we call lift.
  • A Venturi is used as an analogy: fluid speeds up as it passes through a narrowed region and static pressure decreases.

Relative wind

  • Relative wind is airflow parallel to and opposite the aircraft's flight path.
  • The lesson describes the airflow seen by the wing as influenced by the airplane's motion, propeller slipstream/prop wash, and ambient atmospheric wind.
  • More airspeed over the wing increases aerodynamic force, all else equal.
  • Taking off and landing into a headwind gives the wing more airspeed for a given groundspeed, which improves low-speed aerodynamic performance.

Camber and flaps

  • Camber is the curvature of an airfoil.
  • Increasing camber increases the wing's lift capability but also increases drag.
  • Pilots change camber in flight primarily by extending flaps.
  • This tradeoff is useful on approach and landing because the airplane can produce required lift at a lower airspeed while the added drag helps it descend.

Newton's third law

  • The wing also produces lift by deflecting air downward.
  • For the downward change in air momentum, the air exerts an equal-and-opposite upward reaction on the wing.
  • Pressure distribution and downward momentum are compatible descriptions of the same aerodynamic process.

Three practical ways to increase lift

  • Increase airspeed: more airflow produces more aerodynamic force.
  • Increase camber: lower the flaps/change the airfoil shape.
  • Increase angle of attack: orient the chord line at a larger angle to the relative wind, up to critical AOA.
  • Increasing camber or AOA increases drag, and increasing thrust/airspeed can change the airplane's vertical flight path if pitch is not adjusted.

Flight demonstrations / worth watching

  • With the airplane trimmed for level flight, increasing thrust increases airspeed and lift, and the airplane tends to climb.
  • Raising the nose increases AOA and lift demand, but induced drag also rises; with unchanged power the airspeed decreases.
  • Watch these segments if the link between power, pitch, airspeed, lift, and drag is not yet intuitive.

What to remember

  • Airfoil + relative wind produce aerodynamic force.
  • Bernoulli: faster flow ↔ lower static pressure in the lesson's explanation.
  • Newton: the wing pushes air down and receives an upward reaction.
  • Relative wind is opposite the flight path.
  • Flaps increase camber, lift capability, and drag.
  • Three ways to increase lift: airspeed, camber, angle of attack.
