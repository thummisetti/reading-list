Lesson 37 — How to Use VORs

▶ Watch lesson   Back to course

Core idea

  • A VOR defines magnetic-direction radials FROM a ground station.
  • OBS/CDI and TO/FROM let you determine and track a selected course; VOR alone does not provide distance.

Tune — identify — monitor

  • Tune the published frequency.
  • Identify the station by Morse/voice identifier before relying on it.
  • Monitor the signal/flag; do not navigate on an unreliable or unidentified station.

OBS / CDI / TO-FROM

  • OBS selects a course.
  • CDI shows left/right displacement from that selected course.
  • TO/FROM tells whether the selected course points generally to or from the station; it is not a bearing pointer.

Radials and tracking

  • A radial is always named FROM the station.
  • Inbound on a radial uses approximately its reciprocal course, corrected for wind.
  • Intercept with a reasonable angle; once established, crab into the wind to keep CDI centered.
  • Near station passage the CDI becomes sensitive and TO/FROM flips—do not chase the needle.

Checks

  • IFR operations have specific §91.171 VOR-check requirements/tolerances.
  • Even VFR pilots should understand outages/service volume and check NOTAMs.

What to remember

  • Tune—identify—monitor.
  • Radials are FROM.
  • OBS selects; CDI displaces; TO/FROM qualifies the course.
