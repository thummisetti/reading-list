Lesson 37 — How to Use VORs

▶ Watch lesson   Back to course

Core idea

  • A VOR station defines 360 magnetic radials extending FROM the station.
  • The cockpit VOR indicator lets you select a desired course, see left/right course displacement, and determine whether that selected course leads generally TO or FROM the station.
  • The transcript's main teaching strategy is to understand position, bearing, course, and heading conceptually rather than memorize test drawings.

Heading vs bearing

  • Heading is where the airplane's nose is pointed.
  • Bearing describes the direction from one position to another; your bearing/radial from a VOR can differ completely from your aircraft heading.
  • You can fly north while located on a southwesterly radial; position relative to the station does not dictate the direction the nose is pointing.

Radials

  • Radials are always named FROM the VOR station.
  • If you are physically east of a station on the 090° line, you are on the 090 radial, regardless of heading.
  • The reciprocal course (180° opposite) points back toward the station: inbound on the 090 radial is approximately a 270° course before wind correction.

Tune — identify — monitor

  • Tune the published VOR frequency.
  • Identify the station using its Morse/voice identifier before relying on it.
  • Monitor the identification/signal and warning flag. A missing/incorrect identifier or NAV flag means do not use that indication as valid navigation.

OBS, CDI, TO/FROM

  • The OBS rotates/selects the course shown at the top of the indicator.
  • The CDI shows whether the selected course line lies left or right of the airplane.
  • With the CDI centered, the airplane lies on the selected course line (or its reciprocal extension).
  • The TO/FROM flag tells whether flying the selected course would generally take you toward or away from the station. It does not point to the station like an ADF bearing needle.

Finding your radial

  • One basic method: rotate the OBS until the CDI centers with a FROM indication.
  • The selected number then identifies the radial you are on (subject to station passage/cone-of-confusion effects and signal validity).
  • If the CDI centers with TO, the reciprocal of the selected course identifies the radial FROM the station.

Flying TO a VOR

  • Select the desired inbound course so the indicator shows TO.
  • Interpret CDI direction as the location of the selected course relative to the airplane: if the needle is left, the course is left—intercept toward it.
  • Use an intercept angle appropriate to displacement and distance; do not chase the needle with large continuous turns.
  • As the CDI centers, reduce the intercept and establish the inbound course.

Tracking with wind

  • Centering the CDI once does not mean the correct heading equals the selected course. Crosswind requires a wind-correction/crab angle.
  • After establishing on course, note which way the CDI begins drifting and adjust heading a few degrees into the wind.
  • The goal is a stable heading correction that keeps the CDI centered, not repeated S-turns across the course.

Flying FROM / tracking a radial

  • To track outbound on a radial, select the radial/course with a FROM indication and use the same CDI logic.
  • Again, selected course is the desired ground course; actual heading may differ because of wind.

Station passage

  • Close to the station, angular sensitivity makes the CDI move rapidly and indications can become unstable.
  • Directly over/near the station is the cone of confusion; the TO/FROM indication transitions/flips as you pass.
  • Maintain the established heading through station passage rather than aggressively chasing a swinging needle.

DME and limitations

  • VOR azimuth alone does not provide distance. A paired DME/VORTAC or other navigation source can add distance.
  • VOR reception is line-of-sight and depends on facility/service volume, altitude, terrain, and station status.
  • Check NOTAMs for outages and understand that the VOR network is being reduced/modernized; do not assume every charted facility is available.

Reverse sensing

  • If you select a course inconsistent with the direction you intend to fly (for example trying to track inbound while using a FROM setup), CDI corrections can appear reversed.
  • Avoid rote “fly toward the needle” without first confirming the selected course and TO/FROM logic.

VOR checks

  • IFR operations have specific §91.171 VOR equipment-check requirements and tolerances.
  • For private-pilot VFR understanding, know the purpose of VOT/ground/airborne checks and why identification/service status matter even when a formal IFR check is not required for the flight.

What to remember

  • Radials are always named FROM the station.
  • Heading ≠ bearing/radial.
  • Tune — identify — monitor.
  • OBS selects course; CDI shows course displacement; TO/FROM qualifies direction.
  • Inbound on a radial uses its reciprocal course.
  • Intercept, then crab for wind; don't chase the CDI.
  • Expect rapid/ambiguous indications during station passage.
