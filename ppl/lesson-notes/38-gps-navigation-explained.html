Lesson 38 — GPS Navigation Explained

▶ Watch lesson   Back to course

Core idea

  • GPS determines aircraft position from satellite ranging and supports accurate area navigation.
  • Pilot responsibility includes correct waypoint selection, database validity, integrity indications, and a backup plan.

Position solution

  • The receiver measures timing/range information from multiple satellites and solves for 3-D position plus receiver clock error.
  • Satellite geometry and system integrity affect reliability.

Navigation functions

  • Direct-to, flight plans, nearest airports, ground track, groundspeed, ETA, and course guidance are common.
  • Verify waypoint identifier/location/sequence against chart and intended route.

WAAS and integrity

  • WAAS improves GPS accuracy, integrity, and availability through ground monitoring/corrections.
  • Non-WAAS systems may use RAIM; GPS/WAAS outages/interference can be NOTAMed.

Database and overreliance

  • An expired/outdated database may contain obsolete airspace/procedure information and can affect legal IFR use.
  • A moving map does not replace current charts, NOTAMs, outside scan, or positional cross-checks.

What to remember

  • Verify the waypoint.
  • Know WAAS/integrity/outage indications.
  • Keep database current and have a backup navigation plan.
