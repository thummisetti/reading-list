Lesson 15 — Types of Airspace

Core idea

  • U.S. airspace is organized into controlled, uncontrolled, special-use, and other airspace.
  • Controlled airspace classes are A, B, C, D, and E; Class G is uncontrolled.
  • The operating rules, equipment, communication requirements, and VFR weather minima depend on the class and location.

Controlled vs. uncontrolled

  • Controlled airspace means ATC services and specific regulatory requirements apply according to the class; it does not mean every VFR aircraft is continuously controlled.
  • Class G is the portion not designated A/B/C/D/E; VFR pilots remain responsible for see-and-avoid.

Hierarchy

  • When classifications overlap, the more restrictive designation governs.
  • General hierarchy: A > B > C > D > E > G.

Basic VFR weather pattern

  • Class B: 3 SM visibility and clear of clouds.
  • Classes C/D/E below 10,000 MSL: typically 3 SM and 500 below / 1,000 above / 2,000 horizontal ('3-152').
  • Class E at/above 10,000 MSL generally requires 5 SM and 1,000 below / 1,000 above / 1 SM horizontal.
  • Class G minima vary significantly with altitude and day/night; use the current §91.155/AIM table.

What to remember

  • Know which classes are controlled: A/B/C/D/E.
  • Class G = uncontrolled.
  • Requirements change by class, altitude, and sometimes time of day.
  • Use current charts and FAA tables rather than memorizing a single universal rule.
