Lesson 35 — Don't Be Afraid of ATC!

▶ Watch lesson   Back to course

Core idea

  • ATC radio work is structured and predictable: listen, think, transmit clearly and briefly.
  • You do not need perfect phraseology; you do need to communicate accurately and ask when unsure.

Initial call

  • Know who you are calling, who you are, where you are, and what you want.
  • Listen first so you do not block another transmission.
  • At towered airports, include the current ATIS code when appropriate.

Readbacks and clarification

  • Read back runway/hold-short, altitude, heading, and other safety-critical instructions as required/appropriate.
  • If unclear, say “say again” or ask for clarification.
  • “Unable” is appropriate when an instruction cannot be safely or legally accepted; coordinate another option.

Useful terms

  • Roger = received; Wilco = will comply; Standby = wait for controller to return.
  • Do not use “roger” as a substitute for a required readback.

Pilot responsibility

  • VFR ATC services do not remove see-and-avoid responsibility.
  • An ATC instruction does not authorize violating aircraft limitations or regulations; advise ATC when unable or in an emergency.

What to remember

  • Listen first.
  • Who / who / where / what.
  • Read back critical items.
  • Ask when unsure.
