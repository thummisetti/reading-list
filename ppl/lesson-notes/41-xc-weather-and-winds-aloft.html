Lesson 41 — X/C Weather & Winds Aloft

▶ Watch lesson   Back to course

Core idea

  • Cross-country weather planning is a route-and-time problem: departure, climb, en route, destination, alternate/diversion options, and trend.
  • Winds/temperatures aloft affect heading, groundspeed, time, fuel, turbulence, and performance.

Winds/temps aloft

  • Forecast winds are given in degrees true and knots at standard altitude levels; temperatures are included at applicable levels.
  • Use winds near planned altitude to compute wind correction angle and groundspeed.
  • Strong winds can make a large difference to reserve fuel and ETA.

Encoded winds

  • Traditional coded FB groups use two digits direction (tens of degrees), two speed digits, then temperature.
  • For encoded winds 100 kt or more, add 100 to the speed and subtract 50 from the coded direction digits.
  • 9900 means light/variable winds (under the product convention).

Route weather workflow

  • Start broad: synoptic fronts/pressure, GFA, radar/satellite, advisories.
  • Then inspect departure/destination METAR/TAF, route PIREPs, winds/temps, ceilings/visibility, icing/turbulence/thunderstorm risk.
  • Compare observations to forecasts to see whether weather is developing faster/worse than expected.

What to remember

  • Winds aloft are true, not magnetic.
  • They drive heading/GS/time/fuel calculations.
  • Use route-wide weather and trends, not one airport report.
