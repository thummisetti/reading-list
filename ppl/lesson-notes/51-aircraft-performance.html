Lesson 51 — Aircraft Performance

▶ Watch lesson   Back to course

Core idea

  • Aircraft performance depends on weight, density altitude, wind, runway condition/slope, configuration, and pilot technique.
  • Use the actual POH/AFM charts and interpolate correctly; do not substitute generic rules for certified data.

Density altitude

  • Higher pressure altitude and temperature increase density altitude.
  • High DA means reduced engine power (normally aspirated), propeller thrust, and wing performance for a given groundspeed.
  • Expect longer takeoff roll and lower climb rate.

Takeoff/landing charts

  • Start with pressure altitude/temperature, then apply weight, wind, runway surface/slope, and obstacle corrections exactly as chart instructions specify.
  • Ground roll and distance over a 50-ft obstacle are different numbers.

Climb/cruise

  • VX = best angle, VY = best rate; values vary with altitude/weight per POH.
  • Cruise charts trade power, speed, fuel burn, altitude, temperature, and mixture setting.

Margins

  • Published book numbers assume specified conditions and technique; real runway/aircraft/pilot conditions can be worse.
  • Use conservative margins, especially short/soft/contaminated runways, obstacles, high DA, or uncertain wind.

What to remember

  • High/hot/heavy = worse performance.
  • Read chart assumptions and units.
  • Ground roll ≠ obstacle distance.
  • Add realistic safety margin.
