Lesson 45 — Weather Hazards

▶ Watch lesson   Back to course

Core idea

  • Major small-aircraft weather hazards include thunderstorms, wind shear/microbursts, icing, turbulence, low ceilings/visibility, and density altitude.
  • Avoidance and conservative planning are usually safer than attempting to “handle” severe weather.

Thunderstorms

  • Need moisture, instability, and lifting action.
  • Hazards include severe turbulence, hail, lightning, heavy precipitation, icing, tornadoes, and violent up/down drafts.
  • Do not attempt to fly under/through a thunderstorm in a light airplane; use generous lateral avoidance and current guidance.

Wind shear / microbursts

  • Wind shear is a rapid change in wind direction/speed over a short distance.
  • A microburst can produce strong downdrafts and rapidly changing headwind/tailwind components during takeoff/landing.
  • Recognize alerts/PIREPs and avoid; performance can be overwhelmed close to the ground.

Icing

  • Structural icing requires visible moisture and sufficiently cold surfaces/air.
  • Ice changes airfoil shape, increases drag/weight, reduces lift, raises stall speed, and can block probes/induction.
  • Aircraft not approved/equipped for icing should avoid forecast/known icing environments.

Density altitude

  • High, hot, humid conditions reduce air density and aircraft/engine/propeller performance.
  • Expect longer takeoff roll and reduced climb; use POH performance data and margins.

What to remember

  • Thunderstorm = avoid, not penetrate.
  • Microbursts are especially dangerous near the ground.
  • Ice radically degrades performance.
  • High density altitude reduces takeoff/climb performance.
