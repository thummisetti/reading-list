Lesson 49 — How to Fly Cross Country

▶ Watch lesson   Back to course

Core idea

  • A safe VFR cross-country combines preflight planning, weather/NOTAM review, performance/fuel planning, navigation, and continuous replanning.
  • The plan is a baseline, not a commitment to continue when conditions deteriorate.

Preflight

  • §91.103 requires familiarization with available information pertinent to the flight, including weather, fuel, alternatives, runway lengths, and takeoff/landing performance for flights away from the vicinity.
  • Review airspace, TFRs/NOTAMs, terrain/obstacles, airport data, frequencies, and alternates/diversion points.

En route

  • Fly heading/altitude, identify checkpoints, and compare actual time/position/fuel with the nav log.
  • Use pilotage, dead reckoning, GPS/VOR as appropriate, but keep outside visual navigation skills.
  • Get flight following when useful/available, while retaining see-and-avoid responsibility.

Fuel and altitude

  • Meet regulatory VFR fuel reserve and use a larger personal reserve when prudent.
  • Above 3,000 AGL, apply VFR cruising altitude rules based on magnetic course where applicable.

Lost/diversion

  • If uncertain: maintain aircraft control, climb if safe/useful, identify position with chart/GPS/navaids, communicate/ask ATC for help, conserve fuel.
  • Divert early when weather, fuel, daylight, passenger condition, or aircraft performance erodes margin.

What to remember

  • Plan—monitor—update.
  • Compare actual vs expected checkpoint time/fuel.
  • Divert before options disappear.
