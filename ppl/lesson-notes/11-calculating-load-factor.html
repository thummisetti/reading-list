Lesson 11 — Calculating Load Factor

Comprehensive condensed study notes for Josh Campbell / Free Pilot Training.

Core idea

  • For a coordinated level turn, load factor depends on bank angle.
  • The common relationship is n = 1 / cos(bank angle).

Key calculations

  • 0° bank: 1.00 G.
  • 30° bank: about 1.15 G.
  • 45° bank: about 1.41 G.
  • 60° bank: 2.00 G.
  • 75° bank: about 3.86 G.
  • As bank angle approaches 90°, the theoretical load factor required to hold altitude approaches infinity.

Stall speed from load factor

  • Use VSn = VS1G × √n as the basic accelerated-stall relationship.
  • At 2 G, stall speed rises by √2 ≈ 1.414.
  • At 4 G, stall speed doubles.

Important assumptions

  • The 1/cos(bank) formula assumes a coordinated, constant-altitude turn.
  • If the airplane descends, climbs, slips, skids, or accelerates vertically, actual load factor may differ.
  • Use the airplane’s POH/AFM charts where provided.

Exam strategy

  • Memorize the big checkpoints: 45° ≈ 1.4 G; 60° = 2 G.
  • Recognize that the curve gets steep beyond 60°.
  • For stall questions, multiply the original stall speed by √G.
