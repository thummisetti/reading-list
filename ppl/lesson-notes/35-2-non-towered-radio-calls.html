Lesson 35.2 — Non-Towered Radio Calls

▶ Watch lesson   Back to course

Core idea

  • At non-towered airports, pilots self-announce on CTAF to build situational awareness; radio calls do not create right-of-way.
  • See-and-avoid and predictable pattern behavior remain primary.

Frequency

  • Find CTAF on the sectional/Chart Supplement; it may be a UNICOM, MULTICOM, or former tower frequency.
  • Get AWOS/ASOS weather when available before entering the pattern.

Useful calls

  • Common calls: inbound before pattern entry, entering downwind, base, final, departing runway, and clear of runway.
  • State airport name, aircraft type/callsign, position, and intentions; keep it concise.

Pattern and traffic

  • Determine runway/wind/traffic before entry and use standard pattern unless published otherwise.
  • Most patterns are left traffic unless right traffic is indicated.
  • Do not cut in front of established traffic.

Radio limitations

  • Not every aircraft has or uses a radio; a silent frequency does not mean no traffic.
  • A radio call never substitutes for looking outside.

What to remember

  • CTAF coordinates; it does not control.
  • Concise position/intent calls.
  • Expect NORDO traffic.
