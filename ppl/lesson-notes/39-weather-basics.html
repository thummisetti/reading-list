Lesson 39 — Weather Basics

▶ Watch lesson   Back to course

Core idea

  • Aviation weather is driven by unequal heating, pressure differences, moisture, atmospheric stability, and lifting mechanisms.
  • Pilots need to connect weather theory to hazards: low ceilings/visibility, turbulence, thunderstorms, icing, wind shear, and density altitude.

Pressure and wind

  • Air moves from higher toward lower pressure; Coriolis/friction shape the actual wind.
  • Closely spaced isobars indicate a stronger pressure gradient and generally stronger winds.
  • Surface friction slows wind and turns it more across isobars than winds aloft.

Temperature, moisture, dew point

  • Warm air can contain more water vapor than cold air.
  • Dew point is the temperature to which air must be cooled to reach saturation at constant pressure/water-vapor content.
  • Small temperature–dew point spread means high relative humidity and greater fog/cloud potential.

Stability

  • Stable air resists vertical motion: often smooth air, stratiform clouds, haze, steady precipitation.
  • Unstable air favors vertical motion: turbulence, cumulus clouds, showery precipitation, potentially thunderstorms.
  • Lapse rates and moisture determine whether a lifted parcel continues rising.

Lifting mechanisms / fronts

  • Air is lifted by fronts, terrain (orographic), convergence, and convection.
  • Cold fronts often bring narrower bands of more abrupt/convective weather; warm fronts often bring widespread layered clouds/precipitation—actual conditions vary.

What to remember

  • Pressure gradient drives wind.
  • Small T–Td spread = saturation risk.
  • Stable vs unstable weather signatures.
  • Know the lifting mechanisms and connect them to hazards.
