Lesson 60 — Operating an Airplane

▶ Watch lesson   Back to course

Core idea

  • Safe operation depends on understanding the airplane's engine, fuel, induction, ignition, electrical, oil/cooling, and cabin systems well enough to recognize abnormal indications and use the POH checklist.
  • System architecture varies; the actual airplane's POH/AFM is authoritative.

Four-stroke engine

  • Intake → compression → power → exhaust.
  • Power depends on the correct air/fuel mixture, compression, and ignition.
  • Magnetos are engine-driven, self-contained ignition sources; the engine can continue running after a main electrical-system failure.

Fuel / mixture

  • Fuel may reach the engine by gravity feed, pumps, or both depending on design.
  • Mixture controls fuel-to-air ratio; leaning procedures depend on engine/POH and altitude/power.
  • Use the correct tank-selection and fuel-pump procedures and visually verify quantity/quality during preflight.

Carburetor heat

  • Carburetor ice can form in moist air at surprisingly warm outside temperatures because the venturi pressure drop and fuel vaporization cool the carburetor.
  • Carb heat routes warmer unfiltered/alternate air, reducing power initially but melting ice; use per POH/checklist.
  • Fuel-injected engines avoid carburetor icing in the carburetor sense but can still have induction icing/other induction problems.

Electrical

  • Alternator/generator powers the system and charges the battery; battery provides starting/backup energy.
  • Bus architecture, breakers/fuses, master/alternator switches, and load shedding are aircraft-specific.
  • An alternator failure may leave the engine running but progressively deplete the battery and electrical avionics.

Oil / cooling / gauges

  • Oil lubricates, cools, cleans, seals, and protects.
  • Monitor oil pressure/temperature and engine temperature indications for trends; abnormal combinations can indicate serious engine problems.

What to remember

  • Magnetos do not depend on the normal electrical bus.
  • Carb ice can occur in warm, humid conditions.
  • Know fuel, electrical, and abnormal-checklist architecture for the actual airplane.
