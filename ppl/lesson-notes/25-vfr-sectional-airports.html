Lesson 25 — The VFR Sectional — Airports

▶ Watch lessonBack to course

Core idea

  • A sectional encodes airport type, runway layout, lighting, frequencies, airspace, elevation, and basic services in compact symbols.
  • The chart legend and Chart Supplement are the decoding references.

Airport symbols

  • Blue airport symbols generally indicate airports with an operating control tower; magenta generally indicates non-towered airports.
  • Runway patterns depict orientation when scale permits; smaller airports may use a basic circle symbol.
  • Decode unfamiliar markings from the chart legend rather than guessing.

Airport data

  • Typical chart data include airport name/identifier, elevation, lighting notation, longest runway length, and frequencies.
  • CTAF/tower/ATIS/AWOS frequencies may appear nearby; detailed services/hours are in the Chart Supplement.

Currency

  • Read the airport together with nearby airspace, terrain, obstacles, and special-use areas.
  • Use a current chart/database and check NOTAMs because temporary closures, lighting/frequency changes, and runway conditions may not appear on the printed chart.

What to remember

  • Blue usually towered; magenta usually non-towered.
  • Read the data block and legend.
  • Use Chart Supplement + NOTAMs for current details.

Study aid only; use current FAA publications for authoritative information.
