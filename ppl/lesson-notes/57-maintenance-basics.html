Lesson 57 — Maintenance Basics

▶ Watch lesson   Back to course

Core idea

  • An aircraft is airworthy only when it conforms to its approved type design/alterations and is in a condition for safe operation.
  • The owner/operator has continuing maintenance responsibilities, while the PIC must determine the aircraft is safe for the intended flight.

Required inspections

  • Annual inspection: generally required every 12 calendar months.
  • 100-hour inspection: applies to aircraft carrying persons for hire or used for certain flight instruction for hire, with limited overflight permitted solely to reach an inspection facility under the rule.
  • ELT, transponder, altimeter/static, VOR, and other inspections/checks have their own applicability and intervals depending on operation/equipment.

Airworthiness Directives

  • ADs are legally enforceable requirements addressing unsafe conditions.
  • Some are one-time; others recur. Compliance status must be tracked in maintenance records.

Preventive maintenance

  • Part 43 Appendix A lists preventive-maintenance tasks that an eligible certificated pilot may perform on an aircraft the pilot owns or operates, subject to the rule's conditions.
  • The work must be performed properly and followed by the required maintenance-record entry.
  • If the task is not authorized preventive maintenance or is beyond your competence, use an appropriately certificated mechanic/repair station.

Inoperative equipment

  • Do not jump directly from “broken” to “legal to fly.” Use §91.213 and any approved MEL/KOEL/aircraft documents.
  • Determine whether the item is required by type-certification equipment, operating limitations/KOEL, §91.205 or another operating rule, an AD, or the intended operation.
  • If permitted inoperative, it may need to be removed/deactivated and placarded, with maintenance action/recording as required.

Return to service and records

  • After maintenance that may appreciably change flight characteristics or substantially affect operation, §91.407 may require an appropriately rated pilot to make an operational check before carrying passengers.
  • Maintenance records document inspections, AD status, major alterations, and other required history; missing records can become an airworthiness/value problem.

What to remember

  • Annual: 12 calendar months; understand when 100-hour applies.
  • ADs are mandatory.
  • Pilot preventive maintenance is limited to authorized tasks and documentation.
  • For inoperative equipment, use the full §91.213 decision process—not “it isn't in TOMATO FLAMES, so it's fine.”
