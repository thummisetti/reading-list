Lesson 36 — What is ATIS?

▶ Watch lesson   Back to course

Core idea

  • ATIS — Automatic Terminal Information Service is a continuous recorded/digital broadcast of routine terminal information at many busier towered airports.
  • Its purpose is to give pilots weather and airport-operating information before contacting the controller, reducing frequency congestion.
  • Current terminal weather is needed for runway selection, altimeter setting, performance planning, and determining whether conditions permit the intended operation.

ATIS vs automated weather systems

  • ATIS combines weather with terminal operational information and an information code.
  • ASOS/AWOS are automated observing systems focused primarily on weather measurements.
  • At some airports ATIS incorporates data from an automated observing system, but the products serve different purposes.

Typical ATIS content

  • Airport/facility identification and information letter.
  • Time of observation/broadcast.
  • Wind direction/speed and gusts.
  • Visibility and significant weather.
  • Ceiling/cloud information.
  • Temperature and dew point.
  • Altimeter setting.
  • Runways/approaches in use and relevant runway/taxiway/airport operational information.
  • Special instructions, advisories, or items controllers want all arriving/departing pilots to know.

Information code

  • Each ATIS version is assigned a phonetic-alphabet identifier: Information Alpha, Bravo, Charlie, etc.
  • When a new broadcast is issued, the identifier changes. Significant operational/weather changes can trigger an update before the normal cycle.
  • On initial contact, report the current code—e.g., “with Charlie”—so the controller knows you have heard the broadcast and does not need to repeat routine data.
  • If ATC tells you a new ATIS has been issued, obtain it when workload permits or receive the changed information directly as instructed.

Where to find the frequency

  • ATIS frequency is published on sectionals/terminal charts, Chart Supplement, and electronic airport information.
  • The transcript models tuning the published frequency before calling approach/tower.
  • Some modern systems also provide D-ATIS digitally; the operational idea is the same—obtain the current information and identify which version you received.

How to copy ATIS efficiently

  • Listen once for the overall picture, then capture the items needed for the phase of flight.
  • A practical shorthand includes information letter, wind, visibility/ceiling, runway/approach, altimeter, and unusual remarks.
  • Do not let heads-down copying interfere with aircraft control or traffic scan; replay/relisten when practical.

ATIS does not replace preflight planning

  • ATIS is local terminal information, not a complete preflight weather briefing.
  • It does not guarantee all NOTAMs, TFRs, en-route hazards, weather trends, or destination/alternate information have been covered.
  • Use current briefing sources and NOTAMs in addition to terminal broadcasts.

What to remember

  • ATIS = weather + airport/terminal operational information.
  • Listen before contacting ATC.
  • Tell the controller the current information letter.
  • ATIS reduces frequency congestion but does not replace a full briefing/NOTAM check.
