Lesson 4 — What Causes a Spin?

Comprehensive condensed notes for Josh Campbell / Free Pilot Training.

▶ Watch lessonBack to course
Use this as a low-bandwidth replacement for a rewatch. It keeps the instructional points and removes repetition and filler.

Core idea

  • A spin is an aggravated stall combined with yaw.
  • One wing becomes more deeply stalled than the other, so the airplane autorotates in a descending corkscrew path.
  • Spin prevention is largely about avoiding a stall while the airplane is yawed/uncoordinated, especially close to the ground.

Coordinated versus uncoordinated flight

  • In coordinated flight, the airplane turns without excessive sideways acceleration.
  • A slip or skid means the airplane is yawed relative to its path.
  • The inclinometer/slip-skid indicator shows coordination; rudder corrects yaw.
  • With a conventional ball indicator, the common cue is “step on the ball.”

How a spin develops

  • A stall alone does not automatically create a developed spin; there must also be yaw/asymmetry.
  • In a yawed stall, the two wings experience different local airflow/AOA.
  • The more deeply stalled wing produces less lift and more drag, drops, and drives autorotation.
  • The airplane then follows a descending corkscrew path until recovery or ground contact.
  • Skidding base-to-final turns are a classic danger because they combine low speed/high AOA with yaw.

Spin recovery concept

  • Use the specific POH/AFM spin-recovery procedure; some aircraft prohibit intentional spins.
  • A common generic training mnemonic is PARE: Power idle, Ailerons neutral, Rudder full opposite rotation, Elevator forward/reduce AOA.
  • Power reduction removes propulsive effects that may aggravate rotation.
  • Ailerons are neutralized because aileron input in a stall can worsen the asymmetry.
  • Opposite rudder counters yaw/rotation.
  • Reducing AOA breaks the stall; after rotation stops, neutralize rudder and recover smoothly from the dive without overstressing the airplane.

What not to do

  • Do not try to recover a spin with aileron alone.
  • Do not keep pulling aft while the wing is stalled.
  • Do not confuse a spiral dive with a spin; the conditions and recovery techniques differ.
  • Do not intentionally spin an airplane unless the aircraft, limitations, instructor, altitude, and regulations permit it.

Worth watching

  • The animation/diagram showing unequal wing stall is useful for understanding why yaw causes rotation.
  • Watch the coordination/slip-skid explanation if ball position and rudder correction are not yet intuitive.

What to remember

  • Spin = stall + yaw.
  • One wing is more stalled than the other.
  • Maintain coordination and avoid skidding turns near a stall.
  • Generic recovery concept: reduce power, neutralize ailerons, oppose yaw with rudder, and reduce AOA.
  • Always follow the POH/AFM for the actual airplane.
