Lesson 19 — Class E Airspace

Core idea

  • Class E is controlled airspace that is not Class A, B, C, or D and makes up a large portion of the NAS.
  • For ordinary VFR operations, Class E generally does not require ATC communication merely to enter.

Where Class E begins

  • Class E can begin at the surface, 700 feet AGL, 1,200 feet AGL, or another designated altitude; above much of the U.S. it extends upward to but not including 18,000 MSL.
  • Dashed magenta indicates Class E to the surface; shaded magenta generally marks the transition to 700 AGL; shaded blue is used for 1,200 AGL transition areas.

Why it exists

  • Class E often protects IFR routes, approaches, and transitions outside towered terminal airspace.
  • It provides controlled-airspace infrastructure for IFR while allowing routine VFR transit subject to weather rules.

Weather

  • Below 10,000 MSL: generally 3 SM / 500 below / 1,000 above / 2,000 horizontal.
  • At/above 10,000 MSL: generally 5 SM / 1,000 below / 1,000 above / 1 SM horizontal.

Communication/equipment

  • No blanket VFR radio clearance requirement simply for Class E entry.
  • Other rules—transponder/ADS-B, special-use areas, TFRs, or airport-specific requirements—may still apply.

What to remember

  • Class E = controlled but usually no VFR entry call required.
  • Know surface / 700 / 1,200 AGL depictions.
  • Weather minima change at 10,000 MSL.
