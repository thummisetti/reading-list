Lesson 48 — The 60:1 Rule

▶ Watch lesson   Back to course

Core idea

  • The 60:1 rule approximates small angles: at 60 NM, 1° of angular error equals about 1 NM of lateral displacement.
  • It is a fast mental-navigation tool for course correction, descent planning, and position estimation.

Track error

  • Track error angle ≈ off-course distance ÷ distance flown × 60.
  • Example: 2 NM off after 30 NM ≈ 4° track error.

Closing angle

  • To return to course by a chosen future point, closing angle ≈ off-course distance ÷ distance remaining-to-intercept × 60.
  • Total correction ≈ track-error angle + closing angle, then remove the closing portion once back on course.

Other uses

  • 1° ≈ 100 ft per NM (because 1 NM ≈ 6,076 ft and 1/60 ≈ 0.0167).
  • A 3° descent is therefore about 300 ft/NM, useful for quick descent planning.

What to remember

  • 60 NM → 1° ≈ 1 NM.
  • Error angle = off-course ÷ distance flown ×60.
  • 3° path ≈ 300 ft/NM.
