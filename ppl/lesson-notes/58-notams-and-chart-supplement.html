Lesson 58 — NOTAMs & the Chart Supplement

▶ Watch lesson   Back to course

Core idea

  • Charts/publications give the normal published picture; NOTAMs give temporary or recently changed information that can make the published picture wrong for today's flight.
  • The Chart Supplement provides detailed airport/facility information that will not fit on a sectional.

NOTAMs

  • Current FAA terminology is Notice to Airmen (NOTAM).
  • NOTAMs can cover runway/taxiway closures, lighting outages, navaid failures, procedure changes, hazards, TFR-related information, construction, and other time-sensitive operational changes.
  • Read effective times carefully in UTC and determine whether the NOTAM actually affects your route/airport/time.
  • Do not rely on a chart alone for current runway or navaid status.

Chart Supplement

  • Contains airport location/elevation, runways, lighting, communications, services, remarks, traffic-pattern information, tower/FSS data, and other operational details.
  • Use it to answer questions a sectional symbol cannot: hours, runway details, fuel/services, special procedures, and airport remarks.

Preflight workflow

  • Review sectional/chart, Chart Supplement, NOTAMs, TFRs, and weather together.
  • Recheck time-sensitive information near departure; NOTAM status can change after initial planning.

Terminology note

  • The FAA changed the expansion of NOTAM back from “Notice to Air Missions” to “Notice to Airmen” on February 10, 2025. Older videos/publications may use the former wording.

What to remember

  • Chart = normal published baseline; NOTAM = temporary/recent change.
  • Chart Supplement = detailed airport/facility data.
  • Check effective times and recheck before flight.
