Lesson 8 — Using Flaps

Core idea

  • Flaps change the wing’s camber—and on some designs effective area—to increase lift capability at low airspeed.
  • Flaps also increase drag, especially at larger deflections, which makes them useful for steeper approaches without excessive airspeed.

What flaps do

  • Extending flaps increases the wing’s maximum lift coefficient.
  • The airplane can therefore fly at a lower stall speed in the landing configuration.
  • Flaps usually move the nose/pitch trim requirement and create a noticeable change in drag.
  • The exact pitch response depends on airplane design.

Types of flaps

  • Common types include plain, split, slotted, and Fowler flaps.
  • Slotted flaps route high-energy air over the upper surface to delay separation.
  • Fowler flaps move aft as well as down, increasing both camber and wing area.

Use during takeoff

  • Some airplanes use partial flap for short-field/soft-field or normal takeoff; others specify little or none.
  • More flap can shorten ground roll but adds drag and may reduce climb performance after liftoff.
  • Always use the POH/AFM-specified setting and retraction procedure.

Use during approach and landing

  • Flaps allow a lower approach speed and steeper descent angle for a given pitch/power combination.
  • Large flap settings create substantial drag and are particularly useful for landing.
  • Extending/retracting flaps changes lift and drag abruptly enough that pitch and power usually need adjustment.

Flap limitations

  • Do not extend flaps above the published VFE.
  • Abrupt flap retraction close to the ground can cause sudden loss of lift; retract in accordance with the POH and situation.
  • In strong/gusty crosswinds, some aircraft/procedures may use less than full flap—aircraft-specific guidance controls.

What to remember

  • Flaps increase lift capability and drag.
  • They lower stall speed in the extended configuration.
  • Large flap deflections are primarily drag-producing.
  • Use published flap speeds/settings.

Study aid only. Use current FAA publications, your aircraft POH/AFM, and flight instruction for authoritative guidance.
