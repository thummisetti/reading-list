Lesson 26 — The VFR Sectional — Altitudes

▶ Watch lessonBack to course

Core idea

  • Sectionals show terrain and obstacle elevations plus airspace vertical limits.
  • Always distinguish MSL values from AGL descriptions.

Terrain and MEF

  • Contour lines, color tinting, and spot elevations show terrain height.
  • The Maximum Elevation Figure (MEF) gives a conservative indication of the highest terrain/obstacle in a chart quadrangle.
  • MEF is not a regulatory minimum altitude and does not by itself guarantee safe terrain clearance.

Obstacles

  • Obstacle labels commonly show top elevation MSL and height AGL in parentheses.
  • Use the legend for obstacle/group/lighting symbols and remember new/temporary obstacles may be NOTAMed.

Airspace vertical limits

  • Class B shelf numbers are in hundreds of feet MSL: e.g. 100/40 = 10,000 over 4,000 MSL.
  • Class C/D ceilings are likewise charted in hundreds of feet MSL; SFC means surface.
  • Class E transition symbols identify bases such as surface, 700 AGL, or 1,200 AGL.

VFR cruising altitude

  • Above 3,000 AGL, §91.159 generally uses magnetic course: 0–179° odd thousands +500; 180–359° even thousands +500, subject to airspace/other constraints.

What to remember

  • MSL vs AGL.
  • MEF is advisory, not a legal safe altitude.
  • Obstacle top MSL vs height AGL.
  • Shelf numbers are hundreds of feet MSL.

Study aid only; use current FAA publications for authoritative information.
