Lesson 43 — Prog Charts Explained

▶ Watch lesson   Back to course

Core idea

  • Prognostic charts are forecasts of large-scale weather patterns at specified valid times.
  • Use them to understand where pressure systems, fronts, precipitation, and broad flight categories are expected to move—not as a substitute for local/route-specific products.

Surface features

  • High/low pressure centers and fronts reveal the synoptic pattern.
  • Cold, warm, stationary, and occluded fronts have standard symbols; front movement helps anticipate wind shifts, clouds, precipitation, and convective risk.

Forecast precipitation / flight conditions

  • Shaded/colored areas indicate forecast precipitation or weather categories according to the product legend.
  • Always read valid time and legend; symbols vary by product/interface.

Current workflow

  • The current Aviation Weather Center integrates prog-chart views with Graphical Forecasts for Aviation (GFA).
  • Combine prog/GFA with METAR/TAF, radar/satellite, PIREPs, winds, SIGMET/G-AIRMET and trends.

What to remember

  • Prog = forecast, not observation.
  • Read valid time and legend first.
  • Use for the big picture, then drill down to route-specific products.
