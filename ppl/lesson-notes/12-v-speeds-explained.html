Lesson 12 — V-Speeds Explained

Core idea

  • V-speeds are standardized reference speeds for aircraft operation and limitations.
  • The exact values are aircraft-specific; always use the POH/AFM and cockpit markings for the airplane being flown.

Airspeed indicator color arcs

  • White arc: flap operating range; lower limit is VSO and upper limit is VFE.
  • Green arc: normal operating range; lower limit is VS1 and upper limit is VNO.
  • Yellow arc: caution range; use only in smooth air and with caution.
  • Red line: VNE, never-exceed speed.

Common V-speeds

  • VSO: stall speed/minimum steady flight speed in landing configuration.
  • VS1: stall speed/minimum steady flight speed in a specified configuration, commonly clean.
  • VFE: maximum flap-extended speed.
  • VNO: maximum structural cruising speed.
  • VNE: never-exceed speed.
  • VA: design maneuvering speed; varies with weight.
  • VX: best angle-of-climb speed.
  • VY: best rate-of-climb speed.
  • VGLIDE / best glide: speed for maximum glide performance, aircraft-specific.

How VX and VY differ

  • VX gains the most altitude per unit of horizontal distance.
  • VY gains the most altitude per unit of time.
  • With increasing altitude in a normally aspirated airplane, indicated VX generally increases while indicated VY generally decreases until they converge near the absolute ceiling.

Maneuvering speed nuance

  • VA decreases as aircraft weight decreases.
  • Below VA, a full abrupt control input may stall the wing before exceeding the design limit load in the certification sense.
  • VA is not a universal turbulence-proof speed and does not protect against multiple-axis/full-deflection misuse or every gust condition.

What to remember

  • Know the color arcs and their boundary speeds.
  • Know VSO, VS1, VFE, VNO, VNE, VA, VX, VY.
  • Values come from the airplane—not from generic memory.

Study aid only. Use current FAA publications, your aircraft POH/AFM, and flight instruction for authoritative guidance.
