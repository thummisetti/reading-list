Lesson 47 — How to Use an E6B

▶ Watch lesson   Back to course

Core idea

  • The mechanical E6B is two tools: a circular slide rule for proportional/time/fuel/conversion calculations and a wind side for wind correction and groundspeed.
  • The instructor's larger lesson is to understand the relationships well enough to estimate/sanity-check electronic answers.

Calculator-side idea

  • The rotating scales represent proportional relationships. The 60/index position is used for rate-per-hour relationships because 60 minutes = 1 hour.
  • Do not expect the wheel to supply decimal-place magnitude automatically; estimate first. An answer that reads “25” could mean 2.5, 25, 250, etc. depending on the problem.

Time, speed, distance

  • Distance = speed × time; speed = distance ÷ time; time = distance ÷ speed.
  • For a known speed, place the speed opposite the 60-minute index, then read distance opposite elapsed minutes.
  • For unknown time, set speed at the index and find the known distance; the opposite inner scale gives time.
  • For unknown speed, align distance with time and read the rate opposite the 60 index.
  • Sanity check: 120 kt ≈ 2 NM/min; a 60-NM leg should take about 30 min.

Fuel flow / endurance

  • Fuel problems use exactly the same rate relationship: fuel used = gallons/hour × hours.
  • Put fuel flow opposite 60 and read gallons burned opposite elapsed time.
  • For endurance, align available usable fuel with the fuel-flow rate and read time.
  • Do not confuse usable fuel with total tank capacity, and keep legal/personal reserve outside fuel planned for normal trip consumption.

Conversions

  • The E6B includes index marks/scales for common aviation conversions such as nautical/statute miles and kilometers, gallons/liters, and temperature.
  • Weight-volume conversions require the correct fluid density; aviation gasoline is commonly approximated around 6 lb/US gal for planning, but actual aircraft documentation controls.
  • Always track units explicitly; many “E6B errors” are actually unit errors.

True airspeed / density altitude

  • Traditional E6B workflows combine pressure altitude and outside-air temperature to estimate density altitude and convert calibrated airspeed to true airspeed.
  • Use the indicated/calibrated/pressure-altitude assumptions specified by the particular E6B instructions and POH; do not substitute IAS blindly where CAS is required.
  • Electronic avionics may compute these values automatically, but knowing why TAS increases relative to IAS with altitude helps detect implausible outputs.

Wind side — setup

  • Wind direction tells where the wind is from. Rotate the compass rose so forecast wind direction is under the true index.
  • Place a wind dot the appropriate wind-speed distance above the center/reference mark on the grid.
  • Then rotate to the desired true course and slide the grid so the TAS arc/line is set through the wind plot according to the E6B's procedure.

Wind side — answer

  • Read wind-correction angle left/right from the centerline and apply it into the wind to true course.
  • Read groundspeed at the center/reference point.
  • Sanity checks: headwind component → GS below TAS; tailwind → GS above TAS; crosswind from right → required heading generally right of course.

Pressure altitude

  • Pressure altitude can be found by setting 29.92 inHg in the altimeter or calculated from field/indicated altitude and altimeter setting using the standard approximate relationship.
  • Use pressure altitude + temperature as inputs to performance/density-altitude work; do not confuse pressure altitude with true altitude.

Mechanical precision vs operational precision

  • The E6B is an analog calculator; tiny interpolation differences are normal.
  • For flight planning, inputs such as forecast wind and actual fuel flow also have uncertainty. Preserve sensible significant figures and add operational margin rather than pretending a wheel answer is exact.

What to remember

  • 60 index = hourly-rate reference.
  • Distance = speed × time; fuel = flow × time.
  • Estimate decimal magnitude before reading the wheel.
  • Wind side gives WCA + GS from wind, course, and TAS.
  • Track units and usable fuel carefully.
  • Use E6B knowledge to sanity-check electronic planning.
