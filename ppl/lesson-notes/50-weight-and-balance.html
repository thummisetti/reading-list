Lesson 50 — Weight and Balance

▶ Watch lesson   Back to course

Core idea

  • The airplane must be within both maximum weight and the approved center-of-gravity envelope.
  • Being under gross weight does not guarantee acceptable CG.

Terms and formulas

  • Arm = distance from datum.
  • Moment = weight × arm.
  • CG arm = total moment ÷ total weight.
  • Use actual aircraft empty-weight data, not a generic model number.

Forward CG

  • Generally increases longitudinal stability and required tail-down force.
  • Can increase stall speed/required control force and lengthen takeoff, reduce climb, and make flare difficult if too far forward.

Aft CG

  • Reduces stability/control margin and may improve some performance slightly.
  • Too far aft can make stall/spin recovery difficult or impossible because elevator authority/restoring moment is reduced.

Fuel/baggage changes

  • CG can move during flight as fuel burns; direction depends on tank location.
  • Check zero-fuel/takeoff/landing conditions as the POH procedure requires.

What to remember

  • Moment = weight × arm.
  • CG = total moment ÷ total weight.
  • Check both weight and CG for the actual airplane.
