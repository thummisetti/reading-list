Lesson 52 — Required Documents

▶ Watch lesson   Back to course

Core idea

  • Legal flight depends on both pilot documents/qualifications and aircraft documents/airworthiness.
  • For the aircraft, the common memory aid is ARROW, but always verify the current regulatory requirement for your aircraft/operation.

Pilot documents

  • Carry the required pilot certificate and government photo identification when exercising pilot privileges.
  • Meet the applicable medical qualification: FAA medical certificate, BasicMed, or another permitted qualification depending on privileges/operation.
  • Certificates alone are not enough: ratings, endorsements, flight review, and recent-experience requirements must also be satisfied.

Aircraft — ARROW

  • Airworthiness certificate: displayed at the cabin/cockpit entrance so it is legible to passengers/crew.
  • Registration certificate: current and valid for the aircraft.
  • Radio station license: generally not required for domestic U.S. operations, but may be required for international operations.
  • Operating limitations: approved AFM/POH, placards, markings, and supplements as applicable.
  • Weight and balance information: current data for the specific airplane.

Airworthiness responsibility

  • The owner/operator is responsible for maintaining the aircraft in airworthy condition; the PIC is responsible for determining the aircraft is safe for the intended flight.
  • Required inspections, AD compliance, equipment status, and maintenance records matter even if ARROW documents are present.

What to remember

  • Pilot: certificate + photo ID + medical qualification + currency/endorsements.
  • Aircraft: ARROW, current inspections, airworthy equipment.
  • Use the documents for the actual tail number, not generic model data.
