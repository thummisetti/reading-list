Lesson 34 — Digital Instruments

▶ Watch lesson   Back to course

Core idea

  • Glass cockpits combine flight, navigation, engine, and system information electronically.
  • The key pilot skill is understanding data sources, failure modes, reversionary modes, and automation limits.

PFD and MFD

  • PFD typically shows attitude, airspeed, altitude, vertical speed, heading, navigation, and alerts.
  • MFD may show moving map, engine data, traffic, weather, terrain, systems, and checklists.

AHRS and ADC

  • AHRS provides attitude/heading using solid-state sensors and magnetometer inputs.
  • Air Data Computer processes pitot/static and temperature data for airspeed/altitude functions.
  • One sensor failure can corrupt several indications at once.

Trend vectors and automation

  • Airspeed/altitude tapes may show trend vectors predicting near-future values.
  • Flight director/autopilot reduces workload only if modes and targets are monitored.

Redundancy and human factors

  • Know backup battery/bus, standby instruments, and reversionary mode.
  • Keep an outside scan in VFR; do not fixate on menus/moving map.
  • Navigation database currency matters.

What to remember

  • PFD/MFD roles.
  • AHRS = attitude/heading; ADC = air data.
  • Know backup power and failure annunciations.
