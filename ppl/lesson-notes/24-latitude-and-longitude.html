Lesson 24 — Latitude and Longitude

Comprehensive transcript-merged study notes for Josh Campbell / Free Pilot Training.

▶ Watch lessonBack to course

Core idea

  • Latitude and longitude form an imaginary global grid used to identify an exact position.
  • Latitude measures north/south of the equator; longitude measures east/west of the prime meridian.
  • Coordinates are commonly written with hemisphere, degrees, minutes, and optionally seconds.

Latitude

  • The equator is 0° latitude and divides Earth into the Northern and Southern Hemispheres.
  • Latitude reaches 90° N at the North Pole and 90° S at the South Pole.
  • Lines of latitude run east-west and are parallel to the equator.
  • There are 60 minutes in 1 degree and 60 seconds in 1 minute.
  • For latitude, 1 minute ≈ 1 nautical mile. Therefore 1 degree of latitude ≈ 60 NM.

Longitude

  • The prime meridian is 0° longitude and runs through Greenwich, England.
  • Longitude extends east and west to 180°, near the International Date Line.
  • Meridians run generally north-south and converge at the poles.
  • W longitudes increase as you travel west from Greenwich; E longitudes increase eastward.

Accuracy note — longitude distance

  • The video repeatedly treats one minute of longitude as one nautical mile. That is only true at the equator.
  • Because meridians converge toward the poles, the east-west distance represented by one minute of longitude becomes smaller with increasing latitude.
  • Keep the useful rule 1 minute latitude ≈ 1 NM; do not generalize it to longitude everywhere.

Reading coordinates

  • A coordinate such as N 32° 42′ 12″ means 32 degrees north of the equator, plus 42 minutes and 12 seconds.
  • The lesson shows how the hemisphere letter, degree value, minute value, and second value are interpreted in sequence.
  • Seconds can be converted to a fraction of a minute by dividing by 60. Example: 12 seconds = 0.2 minute.
  • Coordinates may also appear in degrees/decimal-minutes or decimal degrees, especially in GPS databases. Confirm the expected format before entry.

Sectional chart scale

  • On the sectional used in the lesson, unlabeled intermediate latitude/longitude grid lines are 30-minute markers.
  • Individual minute tick marks can be counted when estimating a coordinate.
  • Larger tick marks may mark 10-minute intervals, making it faster to interpolate between labeled lines.

Worked written-test example — find an airport's coordinates

  • The instructor locates Libby Airport and first determines hemisphere and the nearest labeled degree line.
  • He then counts minute tick marks to estimate the latitude at about 48°17′ N.
  • For longitude he starts from the nearest labeled meridian and estimates about 115°29′ W.
  • The key exam technique is to work one axis at a time: hemisphere → degrees → minutes → seconds only if necessary.
  • The instructor notes that multiple-choice answers generally should not differ only by a few seconds, so do not over-precision the chart.

Worked example — locate a coordinate on the chart

  • The lesson also reverses the process: start with a supplied coordinate, bracket the latitude between nearby parallels, then bracket longitude between nearby meridians.
  • Once the two lines intersect, compare nearby chart features to identify the requested location.
  • This method is useful for written-test questions asking “what feature is located at these coordinates?”

GPS-entry habit

  • Verify N/S and E/W before entering a coordinate.
  • Verify whether the unit expects degrees-minutes-seconds, degrees/decimal-minutes, or decimal degrees.
  • A coordinate-format or hemisphere error can place the waypoint far from the intended location, so cross-check the plotted point against the sectional.

What to remember

  • Latitude = north/south of equator; longitude = east/west of prime meridian.
  • 60 minutes = 1 degree; 60 seconds = 1 minute.
  • 1 minute of latitude ≈ 1 NM.
  • Longitude spacing shrinks toward the poles.
  • On a sectional, solve coordinate problems systematically: hemisphere → degrees → minutes → seconds.
  • Verify coordinate format before GPS entry.
