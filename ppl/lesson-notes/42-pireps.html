Lesson 42 — PIREPs

▶ Watch lesson   Back to course

Core idea

  • A PIREP is a pilot report of actual conditions aloft—often the best source for cloud tops/bases, turbulence, icing, and conditions between observing stations.
  • Reports are valuable both to other pilots and forecasters.

Typical fields

  • Location (/OV), time (/TM), altitude (/FL), aircraft type (/TP), sky (/SK), weather/visibility (/WX), temperature (/TA), wind (/WV), turbulence (/TB), icing (/IC), remarks (/RM).
  • Not every report contains every field.

Urgent PIREPs

  • Urgent reports (UUA) cover especially hazardous phenomena such as severe/extreme turbulence, severe icing, low-level wind shear, tornado/funnel/waterspout, volcanic ash, or other significant hazards under reporting criteria.

Interpreting

  • PIREP altitude is normally MSL as reported.
  • Aircraft type matters: “moderate” turbulence/icing is subjective and the effect depends on aircraft size/speed/equipment.
  • Check report age and position relative to your route/weather movement.

Giving one

  • Report useful conditions through ATC/FSS or approved electronic means when workload permits.
  • Be precise about position, altitude, time, intensity, cloud layers, and aircraft type.

What to remember

  • PIREP = actual airborne conditions.
  • Location/time/altitude/type give context.
  • Especially useful for turbulence, icing, and cloud tops/bases.
