Lesson 6 — Airplane Stability

Core idea

  • Stability is the airplane’s tendency after a disturbance: whether it returns toward the original condition, stays displaced, or diverges farther away.
  • Stability is discussed about all three axes and is influenced by aircraft design and CG location.

Static and dynamic stability

  • Positive static stability: initial tendency is back toward equilibrium.
  • Neutral static stability: no initial tendency to return or diverge.
  • Negative static stability: initial tendency is away from equilibrium.
  • Dynamic stability describes what happens over time—oscillations may damp out, stay constant, or grow.

Longitudinal stability

  • Primarily concerns pitch about the lateral axis.
  • A conventional tail provides a restoring pitching tendency when the airplane is disturbed.
  • CG position is crucial: a forward CG generally increases longitudinal stability; an aft CG reduces it and can make stall/spin recovery more difficult.

Lateral stability

  • Concerns roll about the longitudinal axis.
  • Dihedral, sweepback, high-wing/keel effects, and weight distribution can create restoring roll tendencies.
  • A sideslip can cause the lower wing to develop more lift, tending to roll the airplane back toward wings-level.

Directional stability

  • Concerns yaw about the vertical axis.
  • The vertical stabilizer acts like a weather vane: sideslip produces a restoring yawing moment that tends to align the airplane with the relative wind.
  • More side area behind the CG generally improves directional stability.

Pilot implications

  • Stable airplanes reduce workload but may require sustained control pressure to hold a displacement.
  • Trim removes sustained control pressure; it does not replace correct attitude/power control.
  • Loading outside CG limits can seriously alter stability and controllability.

What to remember

  • Positive stability tends back toward equilibrium.
  • Forward CG usually = more pitch stability; aft CG = less.
  • Tail/fin geometry and wing geometry provide restoring moments in pitch, yaw, and roll.
