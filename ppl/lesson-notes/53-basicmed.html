Lesson 53 — BasicMed

▶ Watch lesson   Back to course

Core idea

  • BasicMed is an alternative medical-qualification path that allows eligible pilots to exercise certain private-pilot privileges without holding a current FAA medical certificate.
  • BasicMed rules changed under the FAA Reauthorization Act of 2024, so use current FAA guidance rather than memorizing older video limits.

Basic process

  • Meet the general eligibility requirements, including the required medical-history condition and a valid U.S. driver's license.
  • Complete the FAA Comprehensive Medical Examination Checklist (CMEC) and examination with a state-licensed physician at the required interval.
  • Complete an approved online BasicMed medical-education course at the required interval and retain the completion documentation.
  • Continue to self-assess under §61.53; BasicMed does not authorize flying with a disqualifying condition that makes safe operation impossible.

Current operating envelope

  • Under current FAA guidance, the aircraft must be authorized to carry not more than 7 occupants and have a maximum certificated takeoff weight of not more than 12,500 lb.
  • The operation may carry not more than six passengers.
  • Operations may be VFR or IFR, within the United States, at or below 18,000 ft MSL, and not exceeding 250 knots.
  • The flight may not be operated for compensation or hire under BasicMed privileges.

Why the video may be stale

  • Older BasicMed material commonly cites the pre-2024 6-seat/6,000-lb aircraft limits. Current FAA guidance reflects the later statutory expansion.
  • For exam/real-world use, prioritize the current FAA BasicMed page and current regulations.

What to remember

  • CMEC + physician exam + online course + eligibility requirements.
  • Current limits: 7-occupant-authorized aircraft, 12,500-lb max certificated takeoff weight; no more than six passengers; ≤18,000 MSL; ≤250 kt; U.S.; no compensation/hire.
  • Check current FAA guidance because BasicMed has changed over time.
