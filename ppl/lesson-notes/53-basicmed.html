Lesson 53 — BasicMed

▶ Watch lesson   Back to course

Core idea

  • BasicMed is an alternative medical-qualification path that lets an eligible pilot conduct specified operations without holding a current FAA medical certificate.
  • It is not “no medical requirement.” It substitutes a defined eligibility, physician-exam, education, documentation, and operating-limitation framework.
  • The video points students to §61.23; current BasicMed operating limits also appear in §61.113(i) and medical-course/exam details in part 68.

General eligibility

  • Possess a valid U.S. driver's license and comply with its medical restrictions.
  • At some point after July 14, 2006, have held an FAA medical certificate.
  • The most recently issued medical certificate may be expired, but it cannot have been suspended or revoked; the most recent special-issuance authorization cannot have been withdrawn, and the most recent FAA medical application cannot have been completed and denied.
  • Certain specified cardiovascular, neurological, or mental-health histories require the applicable one-time special-issuance process before BasicMed may be used.

Recurring BasicMed process

  • Complete the Comprehensive Medical Examination Checklist (CMEC) and receive the required examination from a state-licensed physician within the preceding 48 calendar months.
  • Complete an approved BasicMed medical-education course within the preceding 24 calendar months.
  • Retain the signed CMEC and course-completion certificate as required; the FAA's current guidance recommends keeping them with the pilot's logbook records.
  • If diagnosed with a condition that may affect the ability to fly, meet the current treatment/care requirements and continue self-assessing fitness before every flight.

Current aircraft limits

  • The aircraft must be authorized under federal law to carry not more than 7 occupants.
  • Maximum certificated takeoff weight must be not more than 12,500 lb.
  • The operation may carry not more than six passengers.
  • These are the current post-2024 limits; older BasicMed material commonly shows a 6-occupant/6,000-lb aircraft limit.

Current operating limits

  • May operate under VFR or IFR if otherwise qualified/equipped/legal.
  • At or below 18,000 ft MSL.
  • At an indicated airspeed not exceeding 250 knots.
  • Within the United States unless the foreign country specifically authorizes the BasicMed operation.
  • The flight may not be operated for compensation or hire under the BasicMed conditions.

Who may use it

  • Current regulations allow qualifying student, recreational, and private-pilot operations under the BasicMed conditions, and qualifying flight instructors/required flightcrew members in the circumstances specified by the rules.
  • BasicMed does not grant a rating, endorsement, currency, or aircraft privilege you do not otherwise have.

BasicMed vs. a third-class medical

  • A current third-class medical and BasicMed are different ways of meeting medical-qualification requirements for qualifying operations.
  • BasicMed can be attractive for pilots who fit its operating envelope, but a conventional FAA medical may be necessary for operations outside that envelope or for other certificate privileges.
  • Know which medical path applies before the flight; do not assume private-pilot privileges automatically mean BasicMed is available.

Current-rule correction

  • The transcript predates the FAA Reauthorization Act of 2024 changes. Any spoken 6,000-lb or six-seat aircraft limit is obsolete for current BasicMed study.
  • For written-test/checkride preparation and real operations, use the current §61.23, §61.113(i), part 68, and FAA BasicMed guidance.

What to remember

  • Prior medical after July 14, 2006 + U.S. driver's license + eligibility conditions.
  • CMEC/physician exam: 48 calendar months.
  • Online course: 24 calendar months.
  • Current aircraft: ≤7 authorized occupants, ≤12,500-lb maximum certificated takeoff weight.
  • Operation: ≤6 passengers, ≤18,000 ft MSL, ≤250 KIAS, VFR/IFR, geographic limits, no compensation/hire.
  • BasicMed does not replace ratings, currency, or the duty to be medically fit for the flight.
