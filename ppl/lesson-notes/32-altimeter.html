Lesson 32 — The Altimeter

▶ Watch lesson   Back to course

Core idea

  • The altimeter is an aneroid barometer calibrated in feet; it converts static-pressure changes into indicated altitude.
  • Correct barometric setting and understanding pressure/temperature error are essential.

How it works

  • Sealed aneroid wafers expand as pressure falls and contract as pressure rises.
  • The Kollsman/barometric window sets the reference pressure.

Altitude types

  • Indicated: what the instrument reads with current setting.
  • Pressure altitude: altitude with 29.92 inHg set.
  • Density altitude: pressure altitude corrected for nonstandard temperature.
  • True altitude: actual altitude MSL; absolute altitude: AGL.

Errors

  • High pressure to low without resetting: true altitude is lower than indicated — “high to low, look out below.”
  • Cold air can likewise make true altitude lower than indicated.

Preflight

  • Set current local altimeter setting and compare indication with known airport elevation within allowed tolerance.
  • A large discrepancy can indicate instrument/static-system trouble.

What to remember

  • Static-pressure instrument.
  • 29.92 → pressure altitude.
  • High→low and warm→cold can put you lower than indicated.
