Lesson 28 — The Heading Indicator

▶ Watch lesson   Back to course

Core idea

  • The heading indicator gives a stable heading reference using gyroscopic rigidity in space.
  • A conventional directional gyro is easier to read than the magnetic compass but drifts and must be realigned periodically unless slaved automatically.

Operation

  • A spinning gyro tends to hold its orientation while the airplane turns around it.
  • Traditional units may be vacuum driven; some are electric. Modern glass systems often use AHRS/magnetometer data instead.

Drift and alignment

  • Friction, mechanical imperfections, and Earth-related effects cause precession/drift.
  • Compare and realign with the magnetic compass in straight-and-level, unaccelerated flight, commonly about every 15 minutes in training practice.

Failure clues

  • Rapid drift, freezing, lagging, or disagreement with compass/other sources can indicate failure.
  • Know the power source because one vacuum/electrical failure may affect more than one instrument.

What to remember

  • Stable gyro reference, but conventional units drift.
  • Align with the compass under suitable conditions.
  • Know the instrument’s power source and cross-check.
