Lesson 27 — The VFR Sectional — Navigation

▶ Watch lessonBack to course

Core idea

  • A sectional is both a map and navigation database: checkpoints, navaids, airspace, frequencies, terrain, obstacles, and route constraints.
  • Good VFR navigation combines chart interpretation with heading/time/fuel planning and continuous position awareness.

Visual checkpoints

  • Choose prominent, unique features such as highways, rivers, railroads, towns, lakes, towers, and airports.
  • Prefer checkpoints easy to identify from altitude and spaced for regular progress checks.
  • Do not rely on ambiguous features, especially in haze/night conditions.

Navaids and frequencies

  • VORs and other navaids use standardized chart symbols and data boxes.
  • Verify navaid service status/NOTAMs before relying on it.

Magnetic variation

  • Isogonic lines show magnetic variation used when converting true course and magnetic course.
  • Keep your conversion convention consistent with the E6B/flight-planning workflow; sanity-check the resulting heading.

Legend habit

  • The VFR Chart User's Guide is part of the pilot's toolkit.
  • When a symbol is unfamiliar, look it up—especially airspace, obstacles, special-use areas, and airport status.

What to remember

  • Use prominent checkpoints.
  • Verify navaids/frequencies and NOTAMs.
  • Account for variation.
  • Never guess chart symbols.

Study aid only; use current FAA publications for authoritative information.
