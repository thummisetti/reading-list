Lesson 14 — Flying in Ground Effect

Comprehensive condensed study notes for Josh Campbell / Free Pilot Training.

Low-bandwidth review notes: preserve the lesson’s core instructional material, definitions, relationships, and practical implications while removing repetition and filler.

Core idea

  • Ground effect is the change in wing aerodynamics when flying very close to the surface.
  • The ground interferes with wingtip-vortex/downwash development, reducing induced drag and increasing effective lift efficiency.

What changes near the ground

  • Downwash and wingtip vortices are reduced.
  • Induced angle of attack and induced drag decrease.
  • For the same AOA/power, the airplane may experience more lift/less drag than it would out of ground effect.
  • Ground effect is strongest within roughly one wingspan of the surface and becomes very pronounced closer than about half a wingspan.

Takeoff implications

  • An airplane may lift off in ground effect before it has enough airspeed to climb safely out of ground effect.
  • If the pilot tries to climb prematurely, induced drag increases as the airplane leaves ground effect and the aircraft may settle back.
  • Soft-field technique deliberately uses ground effect to accelerate while minimizing wheel drag before climbing away at the proper speed.

Landing implications

  • Entering ground effect on landing reduces induced drag and can make the airplane float.
  • Excess approach speed produces a longer float and landing distance.
  • Proper speed control is therefore important, especially on short runways.

Performance and environment

  • Ground effect does not create extra engine power; it changes aerodynamic efficiency.
  • High density altitude, heavy weight, contamination, or obstacles can make a takeoff especially hazardous if the airplane can become airborne in ground effect but cannot climb adequately out of it.

What to remember

  • Near ground → weaker vortices/downwash → lower induced drag.
  • Can lift off before climb capability exists.
  • Can cause landing float.
  • Use proper POH speeds and technique.
