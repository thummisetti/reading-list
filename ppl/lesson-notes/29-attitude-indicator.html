Lesson 29 — The Attitude Indicator

▶ Watch lesson   Back to course

Core idea

  • The attitude indicator shows pitch and bank relative to the horizon.
  • Traditional units use a gyro; modern panels often use solid-state AHRS.

Display

  • The miniature airplane represents the aircraft; the horizon bar represents the horizon.
  • Bank marks show roll angle; pitch marks show nose-up/nose-down attitude.
  • It shows attitude, not altitude or flight path by itself.

Operation and limitations

  • Traditional gyros rely on rigidity in space and may be vacuum or electric.
  • Older mechanical units can tumble or become unreliable beyond design pitch/bank limits.
  • AHRS failures have different indications and annunciations; know the installed system.

Cross-check

  • Confirm attitude with airspeed, altimeter, VSI, heading, and outside horizon.
  • A single display can fail convincingly; instrument cross-check prevents fixation.

What to remember

  • Pitch + bank reference.
  • Know whether yours is vacuum/electric/AHRS.
  • Cross-check rather than trusting one instrument.
