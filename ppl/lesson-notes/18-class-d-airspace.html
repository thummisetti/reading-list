Lesson 18 — Class D Airspace

Core idea

  • Class D typically surrounds airports with an operating control tower but without the radar/traffic level defining Class B or C.
  • VFR pilots must establish two-way radio communication with the tower before entering.

Shape and limits

  • Usually extends from the surface to a published altitude, often about 2,500 feet above airport elevation, but dimensions vary.
  • VFR charts show a dashed blue boundary; the ceiling is shown in hundreds of feet MSL.

Communication

  • As with Class C, hearing your callsign in the tower response establishes two-way communications unless otherwise instructed.
  • Remain outside until communication is established.

Weather

  • Basic VFR: 3 SM and 500 below / 1,000 above / 2,000 horizontal.
  • Special VFR may be available under applicable rules and ATC clearance.

Tower closure

  • Some Class D airspace changes classification when the tower is closed; chart supplement/NOTAM information determines the actual status.
  • Do not assume the dashed circle is always active Class D.

What to remember

  • Dashed blue boundary.
  • Two-way communication before entry.
  • 3-152 weather minima.
  • Check tower operating hours and chart supplement.
