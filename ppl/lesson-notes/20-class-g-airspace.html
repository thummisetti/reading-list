Lesson 20 — Class G Airspace

Comprehensive condensed study notes for Josh Campbell / Free Pilot Training.

Low-bandwidth review notes: preserve the lesson’s core instructional material, definitions, relationships, and practical implications while removing repetition and filler.

Core idea

  • Class G is uncontrolled airspace: the portion not designated A/B/C/D/E.
  • VFR pilots can operate without an ATC entry clearance, but must comply with VFR weather, altitude, right-of-way, and other regulations.

Where found

  • Often exists from the surface up to the base of overlying Class E, especially in less-populated areas.
  • The exact ceiling varies; determine it from the sectional and airspace depiction.

Weather minima

  • Class G VFR minima vary by altitude and day/night and are intentionally less uniform than controlled-airspace minima.
  • Below 1,200 AGL during the day, the well-known airplane minimum is 1 SM and clear of clouds.
  • At night and at higher altitudes, stricter visibility/cloud clearances apply; use the current §91.155/AIM table.

ATC and IFR

  • 'Uncontrolled' does not mean 'no rules.'
  • IFR flight can occur in Class G subject to applicable IFR requirements; see-and-avoid remains vital for VFR pilots.

What to remember

  • Class G = uncontrolled, not unregulated.
  • Know where overlying Class E begins.
  • Weather minima depend on altitude and day/night.
