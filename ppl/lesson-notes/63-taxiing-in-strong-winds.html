Lesson 63 — Taxiing in Strong Winds

▶ Watch lesson   Back to course

Core idea

  • Wind can lift a wing or tail while taxiing. Position the flight controls so the wind tends to push the surfaces toward the ground rather than lift them.
  • Control positions change continuously as your taxi direction changes relative to the wind.

Quartering headwind

  • Aileron into the wind: turn the yoke/stick toward the upwind wing.
  • For a conventional tricycle-gear trainer, elevator is generally neutral or appropriately aft per POH/conditions; aircraft-specific guidance controls.
  • The key is to prevent the upwind wing from being lifted.

Quartering tailwind

  • Aileron away from the wind.
  • Elevator generally down/forward for a tricycle-gear airplane to reduce the chance of the tail being lifted, subject to POH guidance.
  • Memory aid often taught: “climb into a headwind; dive away from a tailwind.”

Taxi technique

  • Taxi slowly enough to retain directional control and stop promptly.
  • Use rudder/nosewheel steering and brakes smoothly; avoid riding brakes with excess power.
  • Strong gusts can exceed practical taxi-control authority; parking/holding position may be safer than continuing.

Tailwheel / aircraft differences

  • Elevator positioning can differ for tailwheel airplanes and particular wind directions/strengths. Use the airplane's POH and instructor technique rather than applying a tricycle-gear mnemonic blindly.

Worth watching

  • This is a visual/kinesthetic topic. Watch the control-position demonstration at least once, then use the headwind/tailwind rules for review.

What to remember

  • Headwind: aileron into wind.
  • Tailwind: aileron away from wind.
  • “Climb into; dive away” is a useful tricycle-gear memory aid, but POH/aircraft type controls.
  • Move controls as taxi direction changes.
