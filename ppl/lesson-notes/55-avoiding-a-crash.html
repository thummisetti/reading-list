Lesson 55 — Avoiding a Crash

▶ Watch lesson   Back to course

Core idea

  • Collision avoidance combines see-and-avoid, right-of-way rules, predictable maneuvering, appropriate altitudes, lighting, and disciplined scanning.
  • ATC traffic advisories are helpful but do not remove the VFR pilot's responsibility to see and avoid other aircraft.

Visual scanning

  • Use short, systematic visual sectors rather than one continuous sweep; allow the eyes to focus at distance.
  • Clear the area before turns, climbs, descents, and maneuvers; pay special attention near airports and practice areas.
  • Aircraft on a constant bearing with decreasing range are a collision threat; little apparent relative motion can be especially dangerous.

Right-of-way fundamentals

  • An aircraft in distress has priority.
  • When converging at similar altitude, the aircraft to the other's right generally has right-of-way, subject to category-specific hierarchy.
  • When approaching head-on, each alters course to the right.
  • An overtaking aircraft passes well clear to the right.
  • Aircraft on final/landing generally have right-of-way over aircraft in flight or on the surface, but may not cut in front of another aircraft already on final.

Minimum safe altitudes

  • Except for takeoff/landing, remain high enough to allow an emergency landing without undue hazard if power fails.
  • Over congested areas, §91.119 generally requires 1,000 feet above the highest obstacle within 2,000 feet horizontally.
  • Elsewhere, generally remain 500 feet from persons, vessels, vehicles, and structures, with specific rules over open water/sparsely populated areas.

Lights and visibility

  • Use required position/anti-collision lights under the applicable rules; lights improve conspicuity but do not substitute for scanning.
  • Sun glare, haze, blind spots, windshield posts, and cockpit fixation can hide traffic.

What to remember

  • See-and-avoid is always active in VFR.
  • Head-on: both right; overtaking: pass right; converging: aircraft on right generally has priority.
  • Constant bearing + decreasing range = collision risk.
  • Know §91.119 minimum-altitude rules.
