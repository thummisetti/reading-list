Lesson 59 — Aircraft Physiology & Risk Management

▶ Watch lesson   Back to course

Core idea

  • Pilot performance can degrade before the pilot realizes it. Safe flying requires active management of physiology, fatigue/stress, hazardous attitudes, external pressure, and decision-making.
  • Use structured risk tools before and during flight rather than relying on optimism.

IMSAFE

  • Illness, Medication, Stress, Alcohol, Fatigue, Emotion/eating.
  • If one factor is meaningfully impaired, changing/canceling the flight may be the correct decision.

Hypoxia

  • Hypoxia is inadequate oxygen available to body tissues; aviation onset can be subtle and judgment may degrade before obvious symptoms.
  • Symptoms vary by person: headache, euphoria, impaired judgment, visual changes, cyanosis, drowsiness, poor coordination.
  • Corrective action: supplemental oxygen and/or descend as appropriate; know the regulatory oxygen rules and your personal susceptibility.

Carbon monoxide / hyperventilation

  • Carbon monoxide binds hemoglobin and can cause headache, dizziness, nausea, weakness, confusion, and incapacitation. Suspect cabin-heat/exhaust problems; shut off suspect heat, ventilate, use oxygen if available, land.
  • Hyperventilation from stress can mimic some hypoxia symptoms; consciously slow/control breathing while preserving oxygen/altitude awareness.

Spatial disorientation

  • The vestibular system can give false sensations in IMC/night/low visual cue conditions.
  • Trust the instruments rather than bodily sensations if visual references are lost; avoid abrupt head movements and get out of inadvertent IMC using trained procedures/ATC help.

PAVE / 5P / hazardous attitudes

  • PAVE: Pilot, Aircraft, enVironment, External pressures.
  • Reassess risk in flight; weather/fuel/passenger condition and external pressure evolve.
  • Recognize anti-authority, impulsivity, invulnerability, macho, and resignation and apply the corresponding antidote rather than rationalizing risk.

What to remember

  • IMSAFE before flight; PAVE for the whole operation.
  • Hypoxia/CO can impair judgment before you appreciate the danger.
  • External pressure is a real risk factor—divert/land early when margins shrink.
