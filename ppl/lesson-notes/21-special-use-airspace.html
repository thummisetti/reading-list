Lesson 21 — Special Use Airspace

Core idea

  • Special use airspace (SUA) contains activities that need confinement or imposes limitations on nonparticipating aircraft.
  • FAA distinguishes regulatory SUA—prohibited and restricted areas—from several nonregulatory types.

Prohibited areas

  • Flight is prohibited unless permission is obtained from the using agency.
  • Established for security or national-welfare reasons; shown with a P-number.

Restricted areas

  • Contain unusual, often hazardous activity such as artillery or aerial gunnery.
  • Flight is restricted when active; authorization from the controlling agency may be needed.
  • Shown with an R-number and published times/altitudes/controlling agency.

Warning areas

  • Similar hazardous activity to restricted areas but located primarily over domestic/international waters where the U.S. cannot impose the same restriction.
  • Participation is not prohibited by the same regulatory mechanism, but hazards may be significant.

MOAs

  • Military Operations Areas separate/segregate certain military training from IFR traffic and identify concentrated military activity to VFR pilots.
  • VFR pilots may transit an active MOA but should exercise extreme caution and obtain activity status when possible.

Alert areas / CFAs / NSAs

  • Alert areas identify high volumes of pilot training or unusual aerial activity; all pilots share collision-avoidance responsibility.
  • Controlled Firing Areas stop hazardous activity when nonparticipating aircraft approach and are generally not charted.
  • National Security Areas request pilots voluntarily avoid flight; mandatory restrictions may be issued when needed.

What to remember

  • Prohibited/restricted = regulatory SUA.
  • MOA/warning/alert/CFA/NSA are nonregulatory SUA categories.
  • Always check active times and controlling agency/status.
