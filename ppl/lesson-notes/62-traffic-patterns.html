Lesson 62 — Traffic Patterns

▶ Watch lesson   Back to course

Core idea

  • The standard traffic pattern organizes aircraft into predictable upwind/departure, crosswind, downwind, base, and final legs.
  • Predictability, spacing, communication, and collision avoidance matter more than drawing a perfect rectangle.

Direction and altitude

  • Most patterns use left turns unless right traffic is published/indicated.
  • Traffic-pattern altitude is airport-specific; check Chart Supplement/airport information rather than assuming a universal 1,000 AGL.

Entry / sequence

  • At non-towered airports, standard practice is to approach/enter in a way that observes traffic and integrates predictably; a common entry is around 45° to the downwind at pattern altitude when appropriate.
  • At towered airports, comply with ATC sequencing/entry instructions.
  • Do not descend into the pattern blindly or cut off traffic already established.

Wind correction

  • Crab on each leg as needed to maintain a rectangular ground track.
  • Tailwind/headwind changes groundspeed and therefore spacing/timing of turns.
  • Use wind indicators and current weather to select/verify the active runway.

Wake turbulence / spacing

  • Stay aware of larger/heavier aircraft and vortices; avoid taking off/landing in the likely wake path.
  • Maintain enough spacing to avoid rushed base-to-final turns.

Base-to-final hazard

  • Do not overshoot final and then use excessive inside rudder to force the nose around while holding opposite aileron.
  • A skidding, high-AOA base-to-final turn can lead quickly to a spin at low altitude.
  • Go around if alignment/spacing is poor.

Worth watching

  • Watch the pattern diagram/visual sequence once if the leg names and geometry are new; text is usually enough for later review.

What to remember

  • Left traffic unless published otherwise.
  • Check actual pattern altitude.
  • Integrate predictably and maintain spacing.
  • Never salvage a base-to-final overshoot with a skid—go around.
