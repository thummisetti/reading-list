Lesson 23 — What is Zulu Time?

▶ Watch lessonBack to course

Core idea

  • Aviation uses Coordinated Universal Time (UTC), called Zulu time, so ATC, weather products, flight plans, and pilots share one unambiguous time reference.
  • Zulu avoids confusion from local time zones and daylight-saving changes.

Reading aviation times

  • Zulu uses a 24-hour clock and is often followed by Z: 1830Z = 18:30 UTC.
  • 0000Z is midnight UTC; 1200Z is noon UTC.
  • Weather reports/forecasts, NOTAMs, and many aviation publications use Zulu.

Converting local time

  • Add/subtract the local UTC offset; account for daylight saving when applicable.
  • When conversion crosses 2400/0000, also change the calendar date.
  • A date-time group such as 121753Z means the 12th day at 17:53 UTC.

What to remember

  • Zulu = UTC.
  • Use 24-hour time.
  • Know your UTC offset and whether daylight saving applies.
  • Watch the date when crossing midnight.

Study aid only; use current FAA publications for authoritative information.
