Lesson 56 — Safety Regulations

▶ Watch lesson   Back to course

Core idea

  • Part 91 safety rules set hard legal boundaries around careless operation, alcohol/drugs, objects, restraints, speed, ELTs, and supplemental oxygen.
  • Personal minimums should often be more conservative than the legal minimum.

Alcohol and drugs

  • Do not act as a crewmember within 8 hours after drinking alcohol, while under the influence, with blood alcohol concentration at/above the regulatory limit, or while using a drug that affects safety.
  • “8 hours bottle to throttle” is a legal floor, not proof of fitness; impairment can outlast detectable alcohol.

Dropping objects

  • Dropping an object is not categorically prohibited, but it may not create a hazard to persons or property.

Seat belts / restraints

  • PIC must ensure passengers are briefed on restraint use.
  • Required occupants must use seat belts/shoulder harnesses as specified during movement, takeoff, and landing, subject to regulatory exceptions.

Aircraft speed

  • Observe the general 250-knot limit below 10,000 MSL and the lower limits associated with Class B/C/D airport areas where applicable, unless an exception/authorization applies.
  • Aircraft limitations may be lower than regulatory maximums.

ELT

  • Most applicable U.S.-registered civil airplanes must have an operable emergency locator transmitter, with specified inspection/battery replacement requirements and exceptions.
  • After a hard landing/impact or suspected activation, check/reset/report as appropriate.

Supplemental oxygen

  • Above 12,500 MSL up to and including 14,000 MSL, required flight crew must use oxygen for the portion exceeding 30 minutes.
  • Above 14,000 MSL, required flight crew must use oxygen continuously.
  • Above 15,000 MSL, each occupant must be provided oxygen.

What to remember

  • 8 hours is only the minimum alcohol rule; be fully fit.
  • 250 kt below 10,000 MSL is the broad speed ceiling, with lower terminal-area limits.
  • Oxygen: >12,500 for >30 min; >14,000 crew use; >15,000 provide to occupants.
  • Know current ELT/restraint requirements for the actual operation.
